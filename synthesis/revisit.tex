\chapter{Worth Revisiting}
\label{ch:revisit}

An ordered queue, ranked by estimated value per hour spent. Each entry gives a reason and a pointer.

\section*{Highest priority}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The 4-bit failure in the quantization tutorial}, the section headed 8-bit RTN.
  \emph{Reason:} the fastest route to making Alistarh's inverse-Hessian derivation feel necessary
  rather than decorative, since naive rounding visibly destroys a model that 8-bit rounding leaves
  intact. Chapter~\ref{ch:10-efficient-learning} supplies the theory immediately afterwards.

\item \textbf{The Golden Gate steering exercises}, numbers 9 to 11 of the mechanistic
  interpretability tutorial. \emph{Reason:} activation steering end to end in three exercises, and the one technique from the
  week that transfers to any model already wrapped in TransformerLens.

\item \textbf{The two lectures on training networks online.}
  Chapter~\ref{ch:06-continual-learning}, then Chapter~\ref{ch:07-to-be-figured-out}.
  \emph{Reason:} the personal notes flag this explicitly as a goal, and the two lectures that
  address it disagree. Lyle's mechanism first, then Pascanu's objection. Lyle's deflationary point,
  that storing the data resolves the easier half outright, settles more cases than expected.

\item \textbf{Dieleman's spectral autoregression post.}
  \url{https://sander.ai/2023/07/20/perspectives.html}.
  \emph{Reason:} the slide version, Figure~\ref{fig:diffusion-models-3}, compresses the argument
  into four panels. The post carries the derivation, and it is the single most reusable idea from
  the diffusion lecture.

\item \textbf{The PPO and GRPO implementations}, Part~2 of the reinforcement learning tutorial.
  \emph{Reason:} PPO is built with a critic, at full memory cost, and GRPO then removes it in a few
  lines. That ordering is what makes GRPO's appeal legible, and it is the current default for
  language-model post-training.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Second tier}

\begin{enumerate}\setcounter{enumi}{5}
\item \textbf{The Kazhdan--Lusztig experiment.}~\citep{davies2021}, and
  Chapter~\ref{ch:08-ml-for-mathematics}.
  \emph{Reason:} the cleanest available example of attribution output becoming a mathematical
  statement, and a usable template for making a model's internals legible enough to constitute a
  claim.

\item \textbf{The linear recurrent unit derivation, all six steps.}
  \citet{orvieto2023}, and Chapter~\ref{ch:07-to-be-figured-out}.
  \emph{Reason:} an ablation dressed as an architecture, whose negative results are the substance:
  HiPPO is unnecessary, continuous time is unnecessary, and complex numbers may buy nothing but
  compactness. Preferable to a Mamba explainer as a first reading on state space models.

\item \textbf{CKA and the invariance argument around it.}~\citep{kornblith2019}, and
  Chapter~\ref{ch:02-representational-geometry}.
  \emph{Reason:} a small formula with a large amount of thinking behind it. The transferable habit
  is to ask, of any similarity measure, which transformations it has been built to ignore.

\item \textbf{PECNET as a baseline.} Poster B20 of the poster session.
  \emph{Reason:} recorded in the personal notes as a specific thing to try on specific data. The
  only entry in this queue that is an experiment rather than a reading, and therefore the one most
  likely to decay if deferred.

\item \textbf{The eight-episode TD versus Monte Carlo exercise.} Slides 37 to 39 of the
  reinforcement learning deck, and Chapter~\ref{ch:04-intro-reinforcement-learning}.
  \emph{Reason:} five minutes with pen and paper, and it permanently fixes the difference between
  fitting observed data and fitting the model that data implies. Best attempted before reading the
  answer.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Lower priority, but not to be lost}

\begin{enumerate}\setcounter{enumi}{10}
\item \textbf{Cho's computational identifiability.} Chapter~\ref{ch:05-statistics-estimation}, final
  section, and~\citet{bynum2026identifiability}. \emph{Reason:} the most conceptually novel material
  of the week, and the one idea from the summer school most likely to change how a problem is framed
  rather than how it is solved.

\item \textbf{Zero-padding applied to causal attention.}
  Chapter~\ref{ch:09-spectral-analysis-dags}, together with the inferred direction in
  Chapter~\ref{ch:research-directions}. \emph{Reason:} possibly nothing, possibly a paper. Cheap to
  sanity-check on a small causal mask before committing effort.

\item \textbf{Geometric deep learning mathematical prerequisites.} Recorded in the personal notes as
  \url{https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02723}, with the course at
  \url{https://geometricdeeplearning.com}. \emph{Reason:} required for the derivations, not for
  applying the four Gs. Worth the time only if the intent is to derive an architecture rather than
  use one.
\end{enumerate}
